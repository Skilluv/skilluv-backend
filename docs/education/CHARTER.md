# Education domain charter

*To be published at `skill-uv.com/education/charter`.*

This charter states what is required, what is refused, and what a validation
rests on. It is binding: a delivery that departs from it is refused, however
good the teaching was.

---

## 1. What an education delivery is

A delivery is an **artefact somebody can hold you to**: something another
educator can pick up and run, or a record of learning that somebody measured.

Admissible:

- a workshop delivered, with its slides, its exercises and its solutions;
- a cohort run to the end, with its completion rate and its measured outcomes;
- a curriculum published and picked up by a trainer other than its author;
- a series of lesson plans another teacher can run;
- an assessment framework two assessors reach the same grade with;
- a report of what changed for the learners.

Not admissible:

- a slide deck with no notes, no exercises and no solutions;
- a cohort whose outcomes nobody recorded;
- a claim of teaching hours with nothing behind it;
- a curriculum nobody has run;
- anything that names a learner.

The difference is not difficulty. It is verifiability — and, in this domain,
whether it can be published at all.

## 2. Somebody has to have learned something

The first requirement, and the one most often replaced by a proxy.

A session everybody enjoyed and nobody can act on has failed at the only thing
it was for. Evidence means a before and after, a completed project, or a
measured assessment. Satisfaction is a real signal about whether people come
back, and it is reported separately, and it is never offered as evidence of
learning.

## 3. Learner data: the requirement that can refuse a delivery on its own

This is the only domain whose artefacts routinely contain facts about real
people who are **not members of this platform**, are **sometimes minors**, and
**never asked to be evidence in somebody's portfolio**.

A cohort report naming students, a testimonial screenshot with a face, an
assessment spreadsheet, a chat log: each is a delivery that cannot be
published as submitted, whatever the teaching was worth.

The rules, and they are not negotiable:

- **Anonymise at source.** Not before publishing — before writing. A document
  that had names in it and had them removed is one where somebody has to be
  sure they got all of them.
- **Aggregate.** A completion figure is a count. A row is never one person.
- **Consent, in writing, for anything attributed.** A testimonial without it
  cannot be stored — the platform's schema refuses it, rather than trusting
  anybody to remember.
- **Minors: nothing.** No name, no image, no voice, no work sample, no
  assessment. Under-18 learners appear in a delivery as a number and in no
  other way. Where a programme is for minors, say so in the brief before it
  starts, because it changes what may be collected at all.
- **A signed declaration before attestation.** Every delivery that reports on
  learners waits, unattested, until its author states that no identifiable
  learner remains. It is a statement with a name and a date on it, and it can
  be withdrawn.

The detail is in [LEARNER-DATA.md](./LEARNER-DATA.md).

## 4. Another educator can run it

Slides without facilitator notes, exercises without solutions, an environment
only the author can set up: those are a performance, not an artefact. What is
delivered includes timings, what to cut when a session runs long, where people
get stuck, and the setup as it works on a clean machine.

This is the requirement that turns teaching into something a platform can
attest. A session nobody else could run leaves no trace anybody can check.

## 5. Assessment is defensible

Rubrics are published before the work is submitted, specific enough that two
assessors reach the same grade, and there is an appeal process. A grade
somebody cannot contest is a grade nobody should have to accept.

Exercises are designed so that copying does not produce a pass. Where it
happens anyway, it is handled as a teaching problem before it is handled as a
disciplinary one.

## 6. Generative tools

Declared, and accepted. A curriculum drafted with a model and then checked is
a curriculum.

What is not accepted: an exercise nobody ran, a solution nobody verified, an
assessment generated and applied to a real person's work without a human
reading it. In this domain the cost of an unchecked output is paid by somebody
who trusted their teacher.

## 7. What Skilluv attestations are, and are not

An education attestation says: this person delivered this, and a competent
reader of the trade found it sound. Where it rests on a cohort, it also says
the cohort ran to the end and enough of its learners finished, counted from
records rather than from a claim.

**It is not a diploma, a teaching qualification, or an accreditation.** It does
not entitle anybody to teach where a licence is required, and it says nothing
about the qualifications a school, a ministry or an employer may ask for. Every
attestation carries that sentence, in clear, on the page it is printed on.
