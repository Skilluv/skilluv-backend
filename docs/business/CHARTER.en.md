# Skilluv economic charter

What Skilluv sells, to whom, and what it refuses to sell. Written to be held
against us: every rule here is meant to be quotable back at us.

---

## 1. The golden rule

**Enterprises pay. Talents never pay for access to opportunities.**

This is not a commercial policy, it is the condition under which the product
works at all. A developer in Cotonou who has to pay to apply will not apply,
and a platform whose best profiles are the ones who could afford to sign up
proves nothing to anybody.

What it forbids, concretely:

- no subscription to be visible to a recruiter;
- no paid promotion of a profile;
- no fee to apply, to receive a bounty, or to withdraw your money beyond the
  real cost of the transfer;
- no artificial limit lifted by a payment.

### The two exceptions, and why they are not exceptions

**Paid mentoring.** One talent pays another. Skilluv takes a commission on the
transaction. That is not access to an opportunity, it is buying somebody's
time — and the mentor is on the other side of the same rule: they are paid.

**The creator marketplace.** A talent sells what they made. Skilluv takes a
commission. Again the talent is the seller, not a buyer of access.

If either line became a disguised toll — mandatory mentoring to be visible, a
marketplace where you must buy to be seen — it would have to go. That is the
test.

## 2. The seven pillars

| Pillar | What we sell | To whom |
|---|---|---|
| **Talent** | Search, introductions, recruitment | Enterprises |
| **Work** | Bounties, missions, outsourcing, assembled teams | Enterprises |
| **Brand** | Sponsorship, events, content | Enterprises |
| **Data** | Scores, aggregated statistics, licences | Enterprises, researchers |
| **Finance** | Advances, financing, intermediation | Talents and enterprises |
| **Ecosystem** | Certifications, training, creator marketplace | Both |
| **Consultation** | Advisory, audits, guided onboarding | Enterprises |

Six of the seven are billed to enterprises. Finance is the only pillar that
touches a talent's money directly, and it is the one with the strictest rules
(§4).

## 3. Pricing principles

**Transparency.** The commission is shown before the engagement, frozen at the
engagement, and readable after. A commission that changes between the
agreement and the payment is a change of contract.

**Alignment.** Skilluv earns when work is delivered, not when it is posted. A
bounty that comes to nothing earns the platform nothing. That is what stops
the platform having an interest in volume rather than outcome.

**A rate we can say out loud.** 15% on a mission, 10% for those who have
already delivered ten. If a rate cannot be explained to the person paying it
without looking away, it is too high.

**No hidden price in the exchange rate.** A talent paid in XOF is paid at the
reference rate, not at a house rate with a margin inside it.

---

## 4. Commercial ethics

**No dark patterns.** A subscription is cancelled where it is bought, in as
many clicks. An option ticked by default is an option nobody chose.

**No lock-in.** A talent's data belongs to them and is exportable. An
attestation stays verifiable even if the person deletes their account — it is
a proof that was issued, not a feature being rented.

**Opt-in for data.** No personal data is licensed without the explicit consent
of the person concerned, with a share of the revenue for them. See
`docs/business/DATA-LICENSING.en.md`.

**The refusal to sell.** Skilluv does not sell to buyers whose intended use is
mass surveillance, discrimination, or identifying people for a coercive
measure. That list is in the licensing policy, and it is not negotiable case
by case.

## 5. Positioning

Skilluv is a **talent network backed by proof of work**.

What that is not, and why the distinction matters:

**Not a job board.** A job board sells visibility to adverts. Skilluv sells
access to people whose work can be checked, which presupposes that the
checking exists — that is the product, not the packaging.

**Not a school.** A school sells a diploma and its credit rests on its
reputation. Skilluv issues attestations backed by a public artefact only: the
credit rests on the artefact, not on us.

**Not a freelance marketplace.** A marketplace makes people compete on price.
Skilluv makes them visible on proof. Somebody who has delivered twenty times
does not have to drop their price to be seen.

The consequence of this positioning is uncomfortable and deliberate: Skilluv
cannot grow faster than the amount of genuinely proved work passing through
it. That is a constraint, not a defect to be fixed.

---

## 6. What this charter commits us to

A product decision that contradicts a rule in this document is either a
mistake or a modification of this document — never a silent exception. The
modification is public and dated, like the rest.
