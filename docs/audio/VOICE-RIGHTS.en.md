# Voice rights

*To be published at `skill-uv.com/audio/voice-rights` and
`skill-uv.com/audio/ai-voice-policy`.*

> **This is not legal advice.** It is what the platform requires, and what it
> understands of the practice. A significant mission or a dispute warrants a
> lawyer.

---

## 1. A voice is not a file

Under French law a voice is an attribute of the person, protected like their
likeness. It is not merely a performance you deliver: it is part of somebody,
and its use requires consent for **each** use.

In practice: **nothing is implied**. A recording delivered for a game cannot be
used in a trailer, an advert, or another game from the same publisher, unless
the contract says so.

## 2. The four questions, before recording

A voice contract that does not answer these four is not one.

1. **Which medium?** The game, and what else: trailer, storefront, social
   media, paid advertising?
2. **Which territory?** One country, one language, the world?
3. **For how long?** Perpetual, or bounded with renewal?
4. **Exclusive?** May the actor work for a competitor, and from when?

A missing answer gets settled later and settled badly: the client has the
product and the actor needs to be paid.

## 3. Portfolio

**By default the actor keeps the right to show an excerpt.** It is how they get
the next job, and on this platform it is what makes their profile checkable.

Two reasonable limits, to be written down:

- **an embargo** — not before the work is released. Legitimate, bounded, common.
- **a length** — thirty seconds rather than the whole session.

A total ban exists (`buyout`), must be visible, and is paid for. It is never a
footnote.

## 4. Non-compete

The trade's practice is exclusivity **by sector and by duration**, not general:
an actor who has been the voice of a phone brand can reasonably agree not to be
a direct competitor's for twelve months. They cannot agree to stop working.

An acceptable non-compete says: which sector, which named competitors, how
long, and in which territory. A clause that only says "the actor shall not lend
their voice to a competing product" is unenforceable and will be read against
whoever wrote it.

Skilluv does not enforce these clauses: they bind two parties, not the
platform. What the platform does is refuse to let them be implicit.

## 5. Pickups and sequels

Reusing a voice in a later version — an expansion, a remaster, a sequel — is a
**new use**. Two honest ways to handle it:

- provide for it in the original contract, with an agreed pickup rate;
- renegotiate when it happens.

The third — treating it as included — is what produces disputes, and is also
what pushes studios towards synthetic voice.

## 6. Voice cloning: the platform's policy

**Forbidden without the person's explicit, specific, written consent.** No
exception.

Concretely, on Skilluv:

- **you may not** train a voice model on takes delivered here, whatever the
  contract, without a separate written agreement that names it;
- **you may not** deliver a synthetic voice in answer to a casting or a voice
  mission;
- **you may not** publish a demo reel containing a voice that is not yours —
  the only place in the catalogue where the `human_verified` AI policy applies
  to challenges, and it protects the entrant rather than the platform;
- **you may** use a generative tool for a scratch track or a timing guide,
  declared, as long as no real voice is imitated.

### Why this firmly

Cloning is the only technology in this domain capable of taking from somebody
not a contract but their ability ever to get one. A cloned voice is
indistinguishable to a listener, produces without limit, and does not
renegotiate. A platform that lets that happen by default — through a general
clause accepted at sign-up — replaces the trade it claims to support.

A general clause is not consent. An agreement must name the use, its duration,
and what happens at the end.

### If you are asked

It is a negotiation in its own right: its own fee, a duration, a territory, a
clause destroying the model at expiry, and a right of withdrawal. It is not a
line to add to a session contract.

## 7. Blind casting

Castings on this platform hide names until selection, by default. It is the one
place where reputation most directly competes with the thing being judged, and
where the judgement is close to instant. The creator can turn the blind off —
visibly, and it is their right when recasting a returning character.
