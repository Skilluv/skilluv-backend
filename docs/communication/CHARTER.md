# Communication domain charter

*To be published at `skill-uv.com/communication/charter`.*

This charter states what is required, what is refused, and what a validation
rests on. It is binding: a delivery that departs from it is refused, however
well written.

---

## 1. What a communication delivery is

A delivery is an **artefact somebody can hold you to**: something a stranger
can read, watch or follow and judge without taking your word for it.

Admissible:

- a documentation change accepted by a project you do not control;
- a tutorial that has been replayed end to end on a clean machine;
- a talk delivered to an audience, with its recording or its slides published;
- an article, a video, an episode or a stream at a public address;
- a translation reviewed by somebody who reads the target language;
- a whitepaper, an industry report or an external specification, with its
  method.

Not admissible:

- a draft nobody has published;
- a talk proposal that was accepted and never given;
- a tutorial whose examples have not been run;
- a translation nobody bilingual has read;
- a piece whose audience figure is the only thing offered as evidence.

The difference is not difficulty. It is verifiability.

## 2. Four non-negotiable requirements

**Every claim is checked.** This is the domain where a confident error travels
furthest: a wrong tutorial is copied into a thousand projects and the author
never hears about it. Every assertion is true and checkable, and every example
has been executed as written.

**Everything borrowed is attributed.** Text, screenshots, data, code excerpts,
figures. With a reachable link. An unattributed paragraph is not a weaker
piece — it is one the platform cannot publish, in the same way an untraced
sample makes an audio delivery unusable.

**A paid partnership is declared at the top.** Not in a footer, not in a
description nobody expands. Sponsorship is legitimate and hiding it is not.

**The level is announced and held.** Prerequisites at the top, and nothing
beyond them assumed afterwards. The unannounced jump is the first cause of a
reader giving up, and it is invisible to the author.

## 3. Accessibility

Alt text on images that carry meaning. Captions on video. A transcript on
audio. Contrast that survives a phone in daylight.

A resource part of your audience cannot consume was not published for that
audience. On a platform built for a continent where most people read on a
phone over an unreliable connection, this is not an accommodation — it is the
main case.

## 4. Generative tools

Declared, and accepted.

The line is not the tool. It is whether a person checked what came out. A
disclosed piece full of unverified assertions fails this charter; a disclosed
piece whose every claim was tested passes it.

One exception, and it is stricter: research writing is set to
`human_verified`. A reference invented by a language model is indistinguishable
from a real one to a reader who trusts the document, and the whole value of
research writing is that its sources can be followed. Open every link before
you publish it.

## 5. Translation

A translation is validated by a person who reads the target language, never by
a tool and never by its own author. Which language that person reads is a
declaration they sign, and it appears next to every review they make.

The platform keeps no list of the languages it believes exist. A language with
two thousand speakers and no ISO tooling is still a language, and refusing it
would be a statement about its speakers rather than about the translation.

## 6. What ownership means here

You keep the rights to what you write, unless a commission says otherwise in
writing before the work starts. A mission states its licensing scope — what
the client may do with it — separately from its IP terms, because a writer who
keeps the copyright still has to know whether the client may syndicate it or
run it under somebody else's byline.

The full terms are in [IP-AND-DISCLOSURE.md](./IP-AND-DISCLOSURE.md).

## 7. What a validation rests on

A reviewer of the family reads the work against a **public grid**. The grid is
published before you submit; you can read it, and you should.

What a validation says is narrow and exact: this person produced this
artefact, and a competent reader of the trade found it sound. It does not say
the piece was popular, and it never will — an audience figure is a portfolio
entry, marked as fetched or declared, and it is not what an attestation rests
on.
