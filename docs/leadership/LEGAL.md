# Leadership — legal and confidentiality

Backlog: leadership/L-01. Shares a counsel review with the security, design,
game and quality charters.

Nothing here is legal advice. It is the position the platform takes and the
questions counsel has to answer before paid leadership missions open.

---

## 1. Why this domain is the hardest of the five

Every other domain's contributor sees a system. This one sees the
organisation: what it is about to build, what it knows is weak, what it pays
people, and what its staff say when they think it is anonymous.

Three consequences follow, and they shape everything below.

- The **default** is that nothing can be published, and the platform's job is
  to make a career visible anyway.
- The **confidentiality period** outlasts the engagement by longer than
  elsewhere: a roadmap is commercially sensitive for as long as it is
  unshipped.
- Some of what a contributor sees is **other people's personal data** —
  performance conversations, engagement survey responses, departure reasons —
  and none of those people are a party to the contract.

## 2. The three redaction states, and what each one commits us to

**`public`.** The author states the document was already publishable. We take
that at face value: it is a claim about their own material, and the platform is
not the arbiter of what somebody's employer allowed them to publish.

**`anonymised`.** The author has rewritten it and a reviewer has confirmed
that nobody is identifiable. Two things matter about how this is worded:

- We do not claim the document *is* anonymous. We claim a competent reader read
  it and could not identify anybody. That is a smaller and honest claim.
- The reviewer is not liable for a re-identification they could not reasonably
  have foreseen. This needs saying in the terms, because the alternative is
  that nobody agrees to be a reviewer.

**`confidential`.** Nothing is shown. The attestation carries only
`leadership_context` — industry, organisation size, team size, duration.

**For counsel:** the wording of the reviewer's undertaking. What is a reviewer
representing when they confirm, and to whom.

## 3. Re-identification is the risk this domain actually has

The realistic failure is not somebody publishing a document with a company name
in it. It is an anonymised roadmap that names a product, a market, a headcount
and a quarter — and one reader in the industry who recognises all four.

Our position:

- The review grid names this explicitly ("including by a detail only they would
  have").
- A contributor may request retraction of a published artefact at any time,
  without giving a reason, and it is removed from public view — the attestation
  survives with its evidence link disabled.
- A **third party** who believes they are identifiable in a published artefact
  can ask for it to be taken down, and it is taken down while it is looked at.
  Not after: while.

**For counsel:** whether the third-party takedown route needs anything beyond
the general one, given that the person asking is not a user and has no account.

## 4. Personal data inside artefacts

A team health audit contains other people's opinions about their workplace. A
hiring rubric contains assessments of named candidates. A departure reason
contains something somebody said in confidence.

Position:

- Individual-level data never leaves the client. What is submitted here is
  aggregate, and the review grid refuses a submission where a group is small
  enough that an aggregate identifies somebody.
- Interview and performance material is **never** submitted, in any state.
  There is no redaction of it that we would accept, because the risk to a
  third party outweighs any career benefit to the author.
- Cohort data on this platform — who joined, who graduated, why somebody left
  — is visible to the lead and in aggregate on their profile. The individual
  rows are not public, and `leave_reason` is never shown attached to a name.

**For counsel:** the second bullet, as a hard prohibition in the terms rather
than a guideline.

## 5. Intellectual property

A roadmap, a decision record, a ladder or a curriculum authored under a paid
engagement belongs to the client on delivery and payment.

The contributor keeps:

- The right to state the work happened, at the abstraction of section 2.
- The right to reuse **structure and method** — a decision-record template, a
  ladder's shape, a curriculum's sequencing. A method is not a deliverable.

They do not keep the right to reuse the content, the context, or anything that
would identify the client.

**For counsel:** where "structure" ends. A curriculum's sequence is arguably
the substance of it, and a client who paid for one will say so.

## 6. Non-solicitation, and its limit

A people-management engagement means seeing who is unhappy and who is
underpaid. That is unusually close to a recruiting list.

Position:

- **No approach** to a client's staff for twelve months after the engagement,
  in either direction.
- What was learned about individuals is not carried to the next engagement.
- **But**: a clause that stopped a contributor working in an industry, or that
  bound them beyond twelve months, is one we will not host. Non-solicitation
  protects the client; a non-compete would make this domain a trap for the
  people it is meant to serve, most of whom are early in a career and in
  markets with few employers.

**For counsel:** the enforceability of twelve-month non-solicitation across the
jurisdictions the platform operates in, and a fallback where it is
unenforceable.

## 7. Advice, and what a fractional engagement is not

A fractional tech lead or product manager gives advice an organisation acts on.
If it goes badly, somebody will ask who is responsible.

Position:

- The contributor advises; the client decides. Every engagement states this,
  and every decision record produced under one names the client-side decider.
- Skilluv is a marketplace and not a party to the advice. Our liability is the
  platform's operation, not the content of an engagement.
- **A contributor is not a director.** Nothing here should be structured so a
  contributor takes on a statutory officer role, and the mission types are
  worded to avoid it.

**For counsel:** whether "fractional tech lead" can be read as a *de facto*
directorship in any of our jurisdictions, and what wording avoids it. This is
the question we most need answered before those mission types open.

## 8. Skilluv's own community roles

The three roles in migration 0469 — cohort lead, community operations, roadmap
contributor — are volunteer positions with a term. They are not employment,
they are not paid, and the handover document at the end is the deliverable.

Two things need writing down: what a role holder may say publicly on behalf of
the community, and what happens to the access they were given when the term
ends.

## What counsel is being asked for

1. The reviewer's undertaking when confirming a redaction, and their exposure.
2. A third-party re-identification takedown route for people with no account.
3. The prohibition on submitting interview and performance material, as terms.
4. Where "structure and method" ends in a paid deliverable.
5. Twelve-month non-solicitation, and a fallback where unenforceable.
6. **Whether a fractional lead can be read as a de facto director**, and the
   wording that avoids it.

Shared session with the security, design, game and quality charters. Item 6 is
the one that gates opening paid missions in this domain.
